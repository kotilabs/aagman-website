A screener is only as good as the data it runs on. If your filter is checking yesterday's close while the market is already up two percent, you're not screening — you're reading history.

## The two types of lag

**Data lag** is how old the numbers are. End-of-day data is fine for long-term filters, useless for intraday setups.

**Compute lag** is how long it takes your screener to process and return results. A 30-second compute lag is fine for swing trades, unacceptable for scalping.

## Design for your holding period

If your average holding period is minutes, your screener needs live ticks. If it's days, minute-level snapshots are enough. If it's weeks, EOD data is fine.

The mistake is building a fast strategy on top of a slow filter. The entry signal arrives, but the underlying moved while the screener was still thinking.

## What to test

Run your screener on historical data and measure the timestamp of the signal against the timestamp of the data that triggered it. If they're not close, your edge is smaller than you think.

A fast strategy needs a fast screener. Match your data frequency to your decision frequency.
