Selling a straddle feels like a free-money trade when premiums are fat. But fat premium can mean one of two things: the market is pricing in real risk, or the market is overpricing risk. IV rank is the fastest way to tell which one you're looking at.

## What IV rank measures

IV rank compares the current implied volatility of an underlying to its range over the last year. An IV rank of 80 means current IV is higher than 80% of the past year's readings. An IV rank of 20 means it's been lower 80% of the time.

It says nothing about direction. It says everything about how expensive options are relative to their own recent history.

## When selling straddles makes sense

The ideal setup is high IV rank + a range-bound expectation. You are selling expensive insurance into a market you believe will calm down. The premium compensates you for the tail risk.

## When it becomes a trap

If IV rank is low but you sell anyway because "premium looks okay," you're not being paid for the risk. You're just taking it. A sudden expansion in IV can turn a small profit into a large loss faster than the time decay can save you.

## The practical check

Before selling any options strategy, ask: is IV rank above 50? If yes, the odds are tilted in your favor. If no, you're betting on calm in a market that may not be calm.

IV rank is not a strategy. It's a filter. Use it before every short-volatility trade.
