The most common backtest mistake is also the easiest to fix: testing on the same data you used to design the strategy. Every parameter you tweak, every filter you add, every lookback you tune — all of it learns the noise in that single dataset. Walk-forward testing is the antidote.

## What walk-forward testing actually means

Instead of one long backtest, you chop history into overlapping windows. You optimize parameters on an in-sample window, then test those parameters on the very next out-of-sample window. Then you roll everything forward and repeat.

The result is a curve built entirely from periods the strategy never saw during optimization. If it holds up, you have something worth trusting.

## Why most traders skip it

It's slower. It's more work. And it often produces worse-looking numbers than the optimized backtest. That's exactly why it's useful — the optimized backtest was never real anyway.

## A simple starting point

1. Pick a window size (e.g., 2 years in-sample, 6 months out-of-sample).
2. Optimize parameters on the in-sample window.
3. Run those parameters on the out-of-sample window. Record the result.
4. Roll forward by the out-of-sample length.
5. Repeat until you reach today.

If the out-of-sample performance is stable across most windows, the strategy is probably robust. If it collapses in one specific regime, you know exactly when and why.

Walk-forward testing won't save a bad idea, but it will kill a good-looking bad idea before you risk capital on it.
