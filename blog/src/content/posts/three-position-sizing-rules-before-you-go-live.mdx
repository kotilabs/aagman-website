A good strategy with bad sizing will blow up. A mediocre strategy with good sizing can survive long enough to be refined. Position sizing is not an afterthought — it's the bridge between idea and account balance.

## Rule 1: Size for the worst case, not the average case

Your backtest average drawdown is meaningless. Size so that your maximum historical drawdown does not force you to stop trading. If you can't withstand a 30% drawdown emotionally or financially, size for a 15% drawdown.

## Rule 2: Use half-Kelly or less

The Kelly Criterion tells you the theoretically optimal bet size. Full Kelly is too aggressive for real trading because it assumes you know the exact edge. Half-Kelly or quarter-Kelly is more robust to uncertainty.

## Rule 3: Correlation counts

If you run five strategies that all lose money in the same market condition, you don't have five positions — you have one oversized bet. Size each strategy as part of a portfolio, not in isolation.

## The bottom line

Your edge determines if you win over time. Your sizing determines if you're still playing when that time arrives.
