Momentum and low-volatility are two of the most studied equity factors globally. In Indian markets, both have worked — but rarely at the same time. Choosing between them is less about which factor is "better" and more about which regime you're in.

## How the factors behave

**Momentum** buys what is already going up. It performs best in trending markets, especially after a breakout or earnings acceleration. It suffers sharp drawdowns when trends reverse.

**Low-volatility** buys the boring, steady names. It performs best when markets are choppy or risk-off, and tends to lag during speculative rallies.

## The Indian context

Over the last decade, momentum has delivered higher absolute returns in strong years like 2017 and 2021. Low-volatility has protected capital better in years like 2018 and 2022. A blended approach has produced the most consistent equity curve, though it has never been the best performer in any single year.

## The takeaway

Neither factor is a permanent edge. The best long-term portfolio uses both, rebalanced by regime. When breadth is strong and trends persist, lean into momentum. When volatility spikes and leadership narrows, lean into low-volatility.

The question is not which factor wins. The question is whether you have a rule for switching between them.
