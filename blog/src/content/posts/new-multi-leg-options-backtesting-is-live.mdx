Until now, backtesting multi-leg options strategies meant approximating each leg separately and hoping the combined result matched reality. That changes today.

## What multi-leg backtesting means

You can now describe an entire options structure — straddles, strangles, spreads, iron condors — in one prompt and backtest it as a single position. Entry, exit, and stop-loss apply to the combined strategy, not individual legs.

## Combined stop-loss

The most important improvement is risk measurement. Instead of stopping out one leg and leaving another exposed, the stop-loss is calculated on the strategy's net P&L. This matches how you actually manage a multi-leg trade.

## What to test first

Start with simple structures: a long straddle into an event, or a short iron condor in a range-bound market. Compare the combined backtest to your old leg-by-leg approximation. The difference is usually larger than expected.
